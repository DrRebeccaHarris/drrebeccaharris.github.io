About
======

I am an applied social scientist working at the intersection of violence, safeguarding, and public health. My research focuses on violence and mental health, as well as the development and evaluation of evidence-informed interventions to prevent harm.

My work combines quantitative and qualitative approaches, including analysis of large-scale survey datasets, mixed-methods evaluation of interventions, and qualitative research with practitioners and policymakers. Much of my research seeks to understand how systems can respond more effectively to violence and vulnerability, particularly among children and young people.

I have worked across academic, policy, and applied research contexts, including research using the Violence Against Children Surveys (VACS) across low- and middle-income countries and evaluations of multi-agency responses to stalking and domestic abuse in the UK. Alongside my academic work, I have experience conducting research within government and working within NHS mental health services.

Further information about my research interests, publications, and teaching can be found using the links above.

Background
======
I hold a PhD in Social Statistics and Demography from the University of Southampton, where my doctoral research examined childhood exposure to domestic violence and abuse in low- and middle-income countries. I also hold an MSc in Foundations in Clinical Psychology and a BSc in Psychology.

My research and teaching draw on interdisciplinary perspectives from public health, psychology, criminology, demography, and applied social research, informed by experience across academia, government, and healthcare settings.
